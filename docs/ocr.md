# OCR: engines, models, languages and word edges

Read before changing `src-tauri/src/ocr/`, `scripts/fetch-assets.py` or `scripts/fetch-ocr-models.py`, or Configurações › OCR. Moved from AGENTS.md on 2026-11-11; keep it current in the same commit as the change it describes.

- **OCR:**
  - The detector tries det_small on the GPU first. If GPU inference fails, it releases the GPU session and retries on the CPU with det_tiny. Foreground and overlay OCR share that CPU session.
  - Manual mode (2026-10-27 to 2026-10-29; replaced by each engine's own models, below; `migrate` turns its settings into `ocrModels.paddle`): `Settings::engine_plan()` gives an `EnginePlan` (ocr/paddle.rs). With no models chosen it is the automatic plan above, from `useGpu`. Chosen, the detector (det_small or det_tiny) and the device of each model are the user's; a stage that fails on the GPU goes on with the same model on the CPU (`EnginePlan::det_models`). `box_thresh` follows the detector model (small 0.45, tiny 0.4) and the detector's image size its device (1920 GPU, 1280 CPU), which is what the automatic plan always did. The engine cache (`ensure_paddle`), the OCR frame key and `engine_changed` key on the plan. The recognizer models offered stay those that read the language's script (rec_tiny and rec_small have no Cyrillic, Arabic or Hangul in their character lists).
  - MeikiOCR and PP-OCRv6 Manga's detector removed (2026-10-30, at the user's request, after trying them: Manga's detector lost characters over the whole screen, MeikiOCR read oddly there, detector and recognizer; the state before, with MeikiOCR's own detectors in `ocr/meiki_det.rs`, is on branch `backup/tests-2026-10-30-meiki-manga-det` at ba7a2aa). A saved `meiki` engine reads as PP-OCRv6, its `ocrModels` entry is dropped and a custom mix with `rec: "meiki"` reads with PP-OCRv6's recognizer (`migrate`); a saved `manga:det` reads as the engine's own. PP-OCRv6 Manga is now comic-text-detector's lines read by its recognizer (`models::MANGA` lists only the recognizer, 21 MB; `Detector::Ctd` is its own and only detector). `models::remove_retired`, at each warm-up, deletes the downloaded MeikiOCR folders and Manga's detector file. The notes below on MeikiOCR and on Manga's detector are history.
  - (Removed on 2026-10-30, above.) MeikiOCR (2026-10-28, from the user's multi-engine OCR request): `ocrEngine`/`screenOcrEngine` = `meiki` ("MeikiOCR (local, japonês)", offered only while the OCR language is Japanese). PP-OCR's detector finds the boxes as always; `ocr/meiki.rs` (`MeikiRec`, a `BoxReader`) reads them with rtr46's D-FINE character detectors (lines 960×32, at most 48 characters; columns 32×480, 24), and `paddle::recognize_boxes_with` puts each character on the step of a 48-px PP-OCR line where its centre lies, so word gaps, ink snapping, spans, columns and turned lines go through the same `line_from` as PP-OCR's own. Columns are read top to bottom only (no `Turn::Right`). Meikiocr's processing, plus two changes of ours measured on rendered Japanese: the padding is the crop's border median, not black (black padding added a `|`, `一` or `。` at line ends: CER 3.2% → 0.0%, and 1.8% → 0.4% on light text over a dark panel), and lines over 960 columns are read in windows overlapping by 128 instead of being shrunk. The models (31 MB, `ocr/models.rs` `MEIKI`, pinned revision and SHA-256, LGPL-3.0) are not in the installers: they download into the app's local data `ocr-models/` when MeikiOCR is chosen (`download_meiki`, from `warm_engine_in_background`) or with Configurações › OCR › "Modelos do MeikiOCR" › Baixar, and "Apagar" deletes them (`install_ocr_models`/`remove_ocr_models` with `meiki`, `Downloaded` in lib.rs). Not Japanese, models missing or downloading: the lookup fails and the pop-up says why (since 2026-10-29 nothing reads in its place). The recognizer runs where its engine's models say (`Settings::rec_gpu_for`; GPU, falling back to the CPU). On the GPU each model is loaded from memory changed by `meiki::for_gpu` (2026-10-28): DirectML (ORT 1.24.4, the user's AMD Radeon) computed D-FINE's box integral `MatMul` by a 1-D weight wrongly, with no error, so every character came out wrong ("U[" for a whole line) and nothing fell back; any DirectML option (optimization level, metacommands, graph fusion) gave the same, and overriding the initializer at session creation did not help. `for_gpu` gives each 1-D MatMul weight read only by MatMuls whose products only Reshapes read (`onnx::Identity_1829` and `_1507` in the pinned models) the shape [n, 1], editing the protobuf field by field; the file on disk stays the pinned one, and the CPU (and its fallback) reads it as it is. Measured on that PC: DirectML then equal to the CPU on all 15 Japanese lines (codes and scores), 252 ms vs 242 ms a batch of 8 lines (44 vs 17 ms a single line). `real_meiki` checks on CI that both changed models read exactly as the files on the CPU. IndraOCR on DirectML was checked the same day: equal to the CPU on 75/75 lines, no change needed. MeikiOCR's own detector (960×544, at most 64 lines) is not used: a whole screen shrinks to half and small text is lost. Measured (CPU, our rendered set, PP-OCR det_small boxes): Japanese CER 0.0% light / 0.7% dark vs rec_small 0.4% / 0.0%; it reads Latin without spaces, hence Japanese only. CI runs `ocr::meiki::tests::real_meiki` (`--ignored`) with the models from `scripts/fetch-ocr-models.py MEIKI`.
  - IndraOCR (2026-10-28, at the user's request; the hybrid the user chose first, to judge Indra's recognition alone): `ocrEngine`/`screenOcrEngine` = `indra` ("IndraOCR (local, experimental)"). Until 2026-10-29 PP-OCR's detector found the boxes; since then IndraOCR's own LineNet does (below). `ocr/indra.rs` (`IndraRec`, a `BoxReader` like MeikiOCR's, `Reader` in lib.rs) reads them with the recognizer group of the OCR language only (`Group::of`: ja/zh/ko `cjk`, ar `rtl`, the rest `base`, whose model sees a window three lines tall; `rec_world` is not downloaded). As indra_ocr's `crop_line`: padding 0.3 h on the sides, 0.2 h above and below (1.6 h in the base window), grey, ink map (1 − grey, a light-on-dark line read as it is), height 32 × window, right-padded with 0 to a multiple of 8; greedy CTC; a line under mean confidence 0.5 is dropped; combining sequences composed (Hangul jamo). Ours: outside the box is indra's off-page fill (white, black for a dark line), since a `BoxReader` gets the box only (measured as good as with the page around it); no beam search with its language model. Columns are read lying down (untested). The weights' licence (modified AI Pubs Open RAIL-M: no use by entities over US$20M except personal/research, none by Datalab's competitors, outputs under it too) is why they are never bundled nor fetched on their own: Configurações › OCR › "Modelos do IndraOCR" › Baixar asks the user to accept it first, and `install_ocr_models` refuses `indra` without `accept_license`; the LICENSE file is downloaded with the models (`models::INDRA`, tag v1.1.0, commit bd02167f, SHA-256 each, 20 MB). Measured (CPU, our rendered set, PP-OCR det_small boxes): CER 0.0% en/zh, 0.0–1.9% ko, 0.4–1.1% ja, 0.9% pt, 3.5–5.1% ru (it reads ё as е, loses ї/ґ), 12% el (no polytonic); 5–16 ms a line, 2–10× faster than PP-OCR's recognizers. CI runs `ocr::indra::tests::real_indra` (`--ignored`) with the models from `scripts/fetch-ocr-models.py INDRA`.
  - IndraOCR's detector, LineNet (2026-10-29, at the user's request): `ocr/indra.rs` `IndraDet`, `det.onnx` (390 KB) from the same pinned commit, downloaded with the recognizers (`models::INDRA`, now 21 MB); an IndraOCR folder from before gets it at the next warm-up, its licence accepted then (`ModelSet::partly_installed`). As indra_ocr 1.1.2's `detect`: grey (OpenCV's weights), ink 1 − grey/255, long side 1024, padded with 0 to a multiple of 32; output [1, 3, H/2, W/2] = core logit, distance up, distance down; a line is a 4-connected core (logit > 0, at least 2 pixels), its mean top and bottom, its width grown by 0.15 of its height at each end; 8 lines or more with a median height under 9 px read again at min(2048, 1024 × 12 / median) rounded down to 32. Not ported: orientation (orient.onnx), deskew, picture regions, the gutter/table splits; lines are horizontal only (no vertical Japanese). Measured before porting (indra_ocr's own pre/post-processing in Python, our rendered set of 8 languages × 3 sizes, light and dark, on the crop and pasted at native size on a 2560×1440 screen): every line found, DirectML equal to the CPU; on the fixtures with IndraOCR's recognizers, ja CER 0.0% (10 lines), ru 4.0% (3 lines; ё as е, as on PP-OCR's boxes). `real_indra_linenet_finds_the_fixtures_lines` checks those on CI, and on Windows that the GPU's lines equal the CPU's.
  - ONNX Runtime's environment under the "C" locale (Linux, 2026-10-31; the user's IndraOCR said "Nenhum texto encontrado" on every page, maybe since it came): `ensure_ort` creates the environment at once inside `platform::locale::in_c` (a thread-local `uselocale`), not lazily at the first session. GTK sets the process's locale from the desktop's, and an environment created under pt_BR (comma decimals) made LineNet compute wrong with no error (core logit max −3.43 on any input, so no line; 7.75 with 15,086 positive pixels in the "C" locale, same library, same input tensor). Found with the C API on the bundled libonnxruntime.so: only the locale at `CreateEnv` matters. Python, CI and Windows run in the "C" locale, which is why the models read there. PP-OCR's models did not show it.

Detectors (`Detector::id`): `paddle:det_tiny`, `paddle:det_small`, `indra:linenet`; an engine offers only its own (`OcrEngineKind::offers`: IndraOCR LineNet only, PP-OCRv6 and MeikiOCR PP-OCRv6's until MeikiOCR's own comes), the custom engine any, and a page engine any for the block under the pointer. The custom engine has a mix for each selector (2026-10-30, at the user's request): the pointer's `ocrModels.custom`, the whole screen's `ocrModels.customScreen` (never set, the pointer's), read through `OcrEngineKind::CustomScreen`, which `engine_for(true)` gives for `custom` and is never stored; both selectors on it show their rows (`choiceKey` in settings-tab.js), "Estado do motor" one line when the mixes are equal, and each mix's models load and are freed as any engine's (`engines_in_use`). `useGpu` has no switch any more (each model has `CPU | GPU`); it stays the default for engines with nothing chosen.
  - No fallback engine (2026-10-29, at the user's request, to save memory): nothing reads in place of an engine that fails. A page engine's error, a recognizer that cannot load (MeikiOCR off Japanese or without its models, IndraOCR without its models) or LineNet without IndraOCR's models fails the lookup, and the pop-up shows the error (`ocr_pass`, `box_engine`, `finder`). PP-OCR loads only the parts an engine in use needs (`Settings::paddle_use` → `PaddleUse {det, rec, plan}`, `PaddleOcr::load`; a part not loaded holds no session, and would load on the CPU if asked for): its detector for its own engines, MeikiOCR, the custom engine on a PP-OCRv6 detector and the pointer's page engine in the automatic capture (`Settings::engines_in_use`); its recognizer for PP-OCRv6 and the custom engine reading with it. IndraOCR with LineNet, a page engine in a fixed region or over the whole screen, load no PP-OCR at all. ORT is initialized by whichever model loads first (`ensure_ort` in `meiki_engine`, `indra_engine`, `linenet_engine` too). `free_unused_engines` drops the PP-OCR configs `Settings::paddle_configs` does not list, LineNet when no engine in use detects with it, MeikiOCR/IndraOCR sessions no selector reads with, ScreenAI's/OneOCR's helpers and Google Lens's/Bing's HTTP clients (with Lens's last read) when no selector is on that engine. The warm-up loads each engine in use's models, the pointer's last.

  - Fushi's manga line layout (2026-10-30, at the user's request): `ocr/manga_layout.rs`, ported from hajisensai/Fushi (GPL-3.0; `ocr_line_layout.dart`, which reads manga with the same PP-OCRv6 Manga recognizer and measured PP-OCR at 41–92% CER on Japanese manga without its furigana filter). When a pass reads with PP-OCRv6 Manga's recognizer (`Recognizer::Manga`), its detected boxes go through `tidy` before they are cached: boxes that touch (within 0.6 of the thicker one's thickness) form a cluster (Fushi has a block detector instead); per cluster the direction is voted by the clear lines' lengths (1.5× longer than thick); for Japanese, furigana is dropped (thinner than 0.6 of the cluster's length-weighted p75 thickness, or under 0.82 of it and hugging a line ≥1.25× as thick on the furigana side: right of a column, above a row, or below it when a row of text is right above that one, within 0.35 of the thicker line, overlapping along); then pieces of one column or row are joined until nothing joins (overlap across ≥0.5 of the thinner, at most 3× the thicker apart along; side by side only at ≥0.8, one line found twice). Turned boxes are left alone. Not ported: Fushi's block detector (ogkalu/comic-text-and-bubble-detector, RT-DETR-v2) and its reading order (Lookitan's own layout orders lines).   - Freed memory goes back to the system on Linux as on Windows (2026-10-31, at the user's request; `platform/memory.rs`): glibc kept what passes and dropped models freed (one 64 MB heap per thread arena, a mmap threshold that climbs to 32 MB), so Linux showed far more than Windows, whose heap releases big blocks on free.   - ONNX Runtime's CPU arena shrinks after each run, on Windows and Linux (2026-10-31, at the user's request): every model runs through `InferenceSession::run`, which tells its closure whether the session is on the CPU, and `paddle::infer` runs it with `run_options` (`memory.enable_memory_arena_shrinkage = cpu:0` on the CPU, plain options on the GPU, whose memory is the provider's). Without it each session kept its largest run's memory while loaded. Measured here (CPU, 4 threads, a 1920×1080 screen / a 16-line batch vs the pointer's region/line), memory held between lookups, before → after: det_small 273 → 55 MB, det_tiny 214 → 42, rec_tiny 203 → 80, rec_small 508 → 176, PP-OCRv6 Manga 507 → 177, IndraOCR's LineNet 78 → 27, rec 137 → 34, rec_cjk 750 → 62; 0–30 ms more on the large runs, none on the small ones. Not measured: comic-text-detector (not downloaded here), OneOCR (its models are decrypted inside Lookitan); they go through the same path. ScreenAI is not ONNX Runtime and has its own helper process.

- **Word edges (`ocr/ink.rs`), as in Lookupper's "word edges are snapped to the ink of the line":** the rules below are Lookitan's own. Lookupper's own, decoded on 2026-10-21 (REA, not ported):
  - Per line (`0x140949b60`): a line whose words are all CJK gives every character its own box, then the analyzer rejoins them. Otherwise each word is a box. Character boxes are centred on the CTC step, as wide as the mean character pitch, the full crop height; a word's box is the union of its characters'.
  - Ink (`0x14094de10`): the background is the mode of a 64-bin grey histogram (every 2nd column, 4th row). The contrast is where the outer 5% of pixels lie; it needs at least 24. A column's ink is the count of pixels further than 0.3 × contrast from the background, over the whole crop height.
  - Ink word split (`0x140950d00`, not for all-CJK or RTL lines, words of 4 to 64 characters): the blank run nearest the middle of each character pair is its gap. A word is cut where the gap is at least max(2.5 × the word's median gap, 0.75 × the line's median word gap, 0.15 × pitch, 0.25 × crop height), leaving pieces of 2 characters or more.
    - Ported on 2026-10-22: `ink::word_cuts` and `paddle.rs` `split_at_ink_gaps`, which puts a space at each cut, after `split_wide_gaps`.
    - Words there are runs of one class between spaces, CJK (`is_cjk_class`, Lookupper's `0x140950be0`) or not.
    - Only the non-CJK runs are cut; Lookupper's analyzer rejoins CJK characters across a space anyway.
    - RTL lines are left alone.
    - Lookitan's `ink.cols` count ink only in the line's ink rows, where Lookupper counts the whole crop height.
  - Snap (`0x14094da60`): between two neighbouring boxes, the best blank run within 0.4 × crop height of their meeting point (width − 1.2 × distance) becomes the boundary. The line's ends move to its first and last ink within that reach. Boxes that still overlap meet halfway (`0x14094a980`).
  - CTC gives each character only a step centre. `ink::measure` finds the crop's ink: pixels far from the median background, in the run of rows holding the most ink.
  - `ink::spans` moves each boundary between two characters to the widest blank gap between their centres, and the line's ends to its first and last ink. Spaces fill the gap between words.
  - `OcrLine.ink_y` holds the ink rows; word boxes and the pick use them (`text_rows` in words.rs).
  - Boxes keep a little room around the ink (`INK_PAD_Y` 12% of the ink height above and below, `INK_PAD_X` 8% at the sides), never past the detected line box, and neighbours that touch share the gap. Raise these two constants if the highlight still looks tight.
  - Drawn box and hit area are separate, so the tight box does not make words hard to hit. `WordBox.hit` (`OverlayWord.hit`, `[x, y, w, h]`) spans the line's whole detected height and reaches halfway to the neighbouring words, or 0.25 × line height past a line's ends. In the overlay, `.w` is that area and takes the clicks and the hover, and its `.ink` child (`pointer-events: none`) draws the highlight and the cut-out. The pointer mode never used these boxes.
  - `Pick::is_under` tests the drawn box, the same one the user sees highlighted. Before 2026-10-15 it reached a quarter of the line height past the word box, which then spanned the whole line height and half the spaces. That made the space between words and lines count as on a word, so the block outline almost never appeared.
